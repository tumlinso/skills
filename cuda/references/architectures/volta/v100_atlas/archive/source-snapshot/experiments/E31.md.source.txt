**Question:** What sharing, latency and failure boundaries hold on this exact Volta stack?

**Minimal setup:** Use compatible documented MPS/IPC configurations and benign finite workloads in separate clients.

**Sweep:** Client count, resource fractions, work mix and isolated versus shared execution.

**Baseline:** Separate conventional contexts and single-client runs.

**Confounders / correctness:** Current MPS docs may include unsupported newer variants; resource shares do not prove fairness or fatal-fault isolation.

**Access gate:** No deliberate fault injection on shared hardware; preserve process/resource lifetimes.

## Measured coverage (2026-10-06)

Three correctness-passing cases used a size-65,536 finite client pipeline on one GPU: one client, two independent CUDA contexts, and two private MPS clients. Median client latency was 0.009216 ms in all three cases; p95 was 0.010240 ms for the first two and 0.017408 ms for private MPS clients. Concurrent-client profiling/replay was not used.

Primary scope: host [run_e31](../scripts/atlas_host.py#L475) launches the finite [pipeline_kernel](../benchmarks/native/logic.cu#L203); its timeline capture is diagnostic only.

## Meaning through representation and execution

- **Supports:** this exact two-client MPS sample had the same reported median as the single-client/context cases and a higher p95.
- **Design implication (inference):** report client latency distributions for a specified workload and sharing mode; aggregate means alone can hide tail changes.
- **Does not establish:** scheduler fairness, fault isolation, general MPS behavior, or a controlled causal comparison of sharing modes.
- **Original protocol gaps:** client count beyond two, resource fractions, work-mix sweep, aggregate useful work, and deliberate-but-safe failure-boundary characterization were not covered.
