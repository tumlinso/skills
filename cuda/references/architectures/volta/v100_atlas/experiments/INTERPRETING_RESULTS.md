# Interpreting the experiment evidence

The experiment cards are proposed protocols. A current status reports the portion actually implemented in campaign `v100-20261006T145155Z-4bd01216`; it does not mean that every proposed case, sweep, mechanism, or validation in the original card was completed. The original `NOT_RUN_ON_GPU` authoring status, protocol text, and source provenance remain available at [the archived atlas commit](https://github.com/tumlinso/gpu_circuit_bending_atlas/tree/5c1f805db80a81f7476ede8292abba69821d104f). This documentation update did not require a new campaign.

For a compact cross-experiment inventory of unsupported claims, their reasons, and narrower defensible statements, see the [claim-limits index](CLAIM_LIMITS.md) and its [machine-readable companion](claim_limits.json). Both are specific to this campaign and preserve the original experiment status vocabulary.

Read each result through five separate questions:

| Lens | What the evidence can support |
|---|---|
| Mechanism qualification | Whether a stated operation, compiler mapping, or hardware behavior was observed for the tested cases. It does not establish that the mechanism is useful across other shapes or workloads. |
| Representation comparison | Whether equivalent useful work was compared across representations, with correctness and the same input cases. Include preparation, transfer, conversion, materialization, and reuse costs when the user-facing path incurs them. |
| Execution contract | Whether the tested interface or sequence met its stated ordering, progress, visibility, or error-handling conditions. A microbenchmark alone does not establish an application-level contract. |
| Platform evidence | Which GPU, software stack, capability, topology, or telemetry was observed. Read-only capability evidence does not imply permission or support for a restricted operation. |
| Restricted hypothesis | What could not be exercised with the public supported interfaces. `NOT_RUN` keeps the hypothesis open; nearby public experiments do not validate it. |

For each comparison, identify the semantic unit of useful work, its equation or operation, the native mapping and numeric contract, and the complete cost boundary being timed. A shape that fits a native instruction or tile is only a coordinate-width convenience until the mapped operator is shown to preserve the required mathematical invariants and exactness. Check the per-case configuration, correctness checks, metrics, limitations, and sample count in the [portable summaries](evidence/v100-20261006/). The `result_summary` string is descriptive metadata; derive claims from case-level metrics and the cited measurement method. Raw vectors and profiler captures are local under `benchmark_runs/`; their absence from Git is recorded in [campaign provenance](evidence/v100-20261006/provenance.json).

Apply the lens in this order: state the question and operator; define its semantic unit and invariants; ask which representation matches a native operation at the required precision; then account for retained state, reuse, and full-path cost. This is a way to read existing evidence, not a proposal for another benchmark. Examples include set membership/count with packed-word AND and POPC (E11); a structured local operator with shuffle or MMA only where its equation and precision fit (E12, alongside the narrower ownership and numerical probes E09–E10); periodic addressing with a fixed-address view (E23); a partition coupling graph compared with measured communication paths (E18, E20); and native integer addition with native arithmetic (E13, E39). These are useful constraints and pairings, not blanket recommendations. The negative E39 arithmetic comparison does not rule out bit representations for Boolean state. Retaining a representation across a longer pipeline may amortize conversion costs, but that is a design inference; this campaign did not measure a whole-pipeline gain.

Primary observations must be separated from explanation. For example, a measured timing difference is an observation; attributing it to occupancy, instruction issue, cache behavior, or a representation's intrinsic advantage is an inference that needs its own evidence. Compare matched cases, state missing baselines, and include setup and conversion costs. Do not select only winning rows or promote a small median ordering into a general speedup.

Several campaign records need specific interpretation:

- **E23:** each `setup_wall_ms` value is one setup observation per case. It is not a set of thirty repeated timing trials and must not be described as a repeated-sample median. The separate `device_ms` vector contains the repeated timing samples.
- **E33:** the full resident-run vectors cover the longer run, while the reported primary median and p95 use complete native events inside the verified stable interval. Keep the full-vector event medians distinct from the stable-interval estimates. Telemetry energy is a read-only sensor estimate from sequential synthetic runs, not calibrated wall-plug energy or a randomized policy comparison.
- **E39:** compare the complete path, including encode/decode and transfers represented by the tested module; allocation was outside the timed path. The apparent bitplane ordering at size 65,536/reuse 8 is about 0.08%. An independent 10,000-resample unpaired 95% bootstrap interval for bitplane minus native median is −0.010072 to +0.008283 ms and crosses zero. It describes sampling variation within this run, not between-run or machine variation, and supports no established crossover.

`GPU_RUN` indicates that some GPU cases ran and passed their recorded checks; it is not a blanket pass for an original protocol. `CPU_ONLY` describes CPU-side evidence. `COMPILED_ONLY`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, `REJECTED`, and `NOT_RUN` retain their distinct meanings and must keep the reason and tested boundary attached. No result in this campaign is biological validation or support for a biological claim.
